## 💼 Business

### Markets Hit Record Highs; Chip Stocks Lead the Rally
The S&P 500 closed at a new all-time high today, finishing above the 7,600 threshold for the first time. The index advanced 0.13% to end at 7,609.78, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 228.91 points (0.45%) to 51,307.79. The Nasdaq Composite eked out a gain of 0.03% to close at 27,093.90.

**Marvell Technology** surged 25% after Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang called it the "next trillion-dollar company" at Computex 2026 in Taipei. **Hewlett Packard Enterprise** also surged 25% after posting quarterly earnings and full-year revenue guidance that topped analyst estimates.

**Credo Technology** shares, however, plunged 12% despite beating both top- and bottom-line expectations for Q4, posting adjusted earnings of $1.16/share on revenue of $437 million. **Microchip Technology** popped 12% after revealing its data center solutions unit saw ~65% revenue growth projected for this calendar year.

### Alphabet Raises $80 Billion for AI Buildout
Alphabet announced plans to raise $80 billion from stock sales to fund its artificial intelligence expansion, including a $10 billion investment from Berkshire Hathaway. Alphabet shares fell nearly 4% on the news.

### SpaceX IPO on the Horizon — Morningstar Has Doubts
SpaceX is targeting a Nasdaq debut on June 12, with a roadshow set to kick off June 4. Morningstar analysts have pegged SpaceX's valuation at $780 billion — less than half of what the company is reportedly seeking — citing uncertain economics around its AI business (xAI/Grok) and technological hurdles facing Starlink.

### Pre-ChatGPT Startups Being Crushed by AI Wave
A generation of startups founded before ChatGPT is facing a brutal reckoning. Valuations have compressed roughly sixfold from the 2021 peak of 50x forward revenues, meaning a company with the same revenue is worth ~85% less than five years ago. AI coding tools have also eroded the "acqui-hire" floor that once gave startups a valuation safety net.

### Victoria's Secret Raises Guidance, Cites Lower Tariff Costs
Victoria's Secret beat Q1 earnings expectations and raised its full-year sales guidance to between $7.03 billion and $7.13 billion — up from its prior range — citing lower tariff costs as a key tailwind.

## 💻 Technology

### Nvidia Dominates Computex 2026 in Taipei
Nvidia unveiled its **RTX Spark Superchip** at Computex, moving beyond GPUs into full AI PC silicon for laptops and mini-PCs. The chip combines Blackwell RTX graphics with Grace CPU technology and is expected in Windows devices from major OEMs including Microsoft and Dell. The move puts Nvidia directly into the consumer PC stack, challenging Apple, Intel, AMD, and Qualcomm.

Nvidia also selected Unitree as its humanoid robot platform partner, as the Chinese startup eyes its own IPO. Jensen Huang's high-profile endorsements at Computex drove major market moves throughout the day.

### Microsoft Build 2026: Agentic AI Front and Center
Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella took the stage at Microsoft Build 2026, unveiling new Windows AI APIs expanding to more PCs across CPU, GPU, and NPU, and announced new **Aion 1.0 AI models**. Microsoft is partnering with Nvidia on a unified stack for agentic AI deployment, spanning Windows devices to cloud. New Surface devices with Nvidia chips were also teased.

### Apple WWDC 2026 Coming June 8
Apple is ramping up ahead of WWDC 2026, which kicks off with a keynote on Monday, June 8. The company is expected to unveil iOS 27, iPadOS 27, macOS 27, watchOS 27, tvOS 27, and visionOS 27. The event teaser wallpaper features a "Glow all out" tagline, hinting at a redesigned Siri. Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reports macOS 27 will include a "slight redesign" to improve the readability of the Liquid Glass interface introduced in macOS Tahoe.

### Amazon Announces Prime Day 2026: June 23–26
Amazon officially confirmed a four-day Prime Day event running June 23–26, with early deals already available for Prime members. The extended event marks the longest Prime Day window yet, with major retailer partners expected to offer competitive discounts across tech, home goods, and more.

### Data Center Buildout Hits a Wall
Analyses indicate that 30–50% of approximately 140 planned U.S. data centers targeting 16 GW of capacity may miss 2026 timelines or be canceled outright. Primary bottlenecks include multi-year waits for transformers, batteries, and grid connections — alongside growing community opposition over energy and water usage. Ohio suspended a major tax incentive for data centers after costs surged, and residents are pushing a ballot measure that could ban hyperscale data centers statewide.

### U.S. Clamps Down on Nvidia Chip Exports to China
The U.S. Commerce Department issued new guidance aimed at stopping advanced Nvidia AI chips from reaching Chinese companies through overseas subsidiaries, continuing the escalating effort to limit China's access to frontier AI hardware.

### VC Shifts from Software to Hardware ("Atoms over Apps")
Venture capital is pivoting from software to hard-tech. Infrastructure, chips, robotics, and defense tech are gaining favor as AI makes software easier to build and harder to monetize. Eclipse-backed companies alone have raised $14 billion in 2026, including Cerebras' IPO.

### Tech Layoffs Accelerate Amid AI Automation Wave
Tech layoffs in 2026 are already nearly matching all of 2025. ClickUp recently cut 22% of its workforce citing AI agents. Box founder Aaron Levie warned of "AI psychosis" — the tendency for executives to overestimate how easily AI can replace complex human roles.

## 🌍 World Events

### Russia Launches Massive Strike on Kyiv and Ukrainian Cities
Russia launched hundreds of drones and dozens of missiles against Kyiv and other Ukrainian cities overnight, killing at least 16 civilians. Some people were trapped under the rubble of destroyed apartment buildings in what represents one of the more intense bombardments in recent weeks.

### Israel Continues Strikes on Lebanon Despite Trump Ceasefire Claim
Despite President Trump stating that both sides had "agreed that all shooting will stop," Israel's military continued attacks on Lebanon overnight, killing at least eight people. Among the dead was James Karam, a dentist killed along with his daughter and son when an Israeli drone bombed their car in southern Lebanon. Lebanon's Health Ministry reports Israeli strikes have killed more than 3,400 people since March 2, with over 10,000 wounded. An Israeli attack also damaged the Jabal Amel University Hospital in Tyre. Separately, Axios reported Trump told Israeli PM Netanyahu in an expletive-laden call: "You're f'ing crazy. You'd be in prison if it weren't for me."

### Iran-U.S. Negotiations Remain Tense
Oil prices jumped Monday after Iran suspended indirect negotiations with the United States in protest of Israel's military actions in Lebanon. President Trump later posted on Truth Social that indirect talks with Iran were continuing at a "rapid pace," helping to steady markets.

### Six U.S. States Hold Primary Elections Today
California, Iowa, Montana, New Jersey, South Dakota, and New Mexico are all holding primary elections on Tuesday, June 2. Results are expected to shape congressional and gubernatorial races heading into the fall campaign season.

### Trump Taps Bill Pulte as Acting Intelligence Director
President Trump named Bill Pulte — formerly the director of the Federal Housing Finance Agency — as acting intelligence director, replacing Tulsi Gabbard. Pulte has demonstrated a willingness to go after the president's perceived political enemies, raising questions about the direction of the intelligence community.

### Court Blocks Pentagon's Transgender Military Ban
A divided appeals court panel ruled that a Trump administration policy illegally banned transgender troops from military service. However, the court is still allowing the administration to continue barring transgender people from *entering* the military — a nuanced but consequential distinction.

### Florida Sues OpenAI Over Child Safety Concerns
Florida became the first state to file a civil lawsuit against OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman, alleging the company knowingly endangered children through ChatGPT and deceived parents about the app's safety. Attorney General James Uthmeier called the filing a "monumental" civil action.

### Senator Sanders Proposes AI Sovereign Wealth Fund
Senator Bernie Sanders introduced a bill that would create a sovereign wealth fund by imposing a one-time 50% tax on the stock of OpenAI, Anthropic, and other major AI companies. The proposal aims to give the public a direct financial stake in the AI revolution and ensure its benefits are broadly shared.

### Ultra-Orthodox Protests Paralyze Israel Over Military Draft
Tens of thousands of ultra-Orthodox Jews demonstrated across Israel on Monday, blocking roads and trains and setting cars on fire, in protest of mandatory military enlistment. The demonstrations caused widespread disruption across the country.

### Pentagon Press Office Declared a Classified Space
Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth designated the Pentagon press office a classified space, physically barring journalists from an area that has been freely accessible to reporters for decades. Reporters Without Borders condemned the move, pledging continued accountability reporting on the Pentagon.
